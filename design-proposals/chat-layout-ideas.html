Five ways to make Thryve feel like an AI chat product

The chat is the main thing people do here, so these layouts give it the space. Every mockup uses the same brand colors, fonts and pill buttons as the app, and the same example conversation, so you can compare the layout and nothing else. Use the Theme button to see dark mode.

Why today's screen feels compact
  • The top navigation bar and a 1,200 px page frame leave the chat card about 900 px wide and roughly 450 px tall on a laptop.
  • Chat sits inside a bordered card, inside the page, so the composer and messages compete with three layers of borders.
  • Text is 14.5 px, messages are close together, and the composer starts two lines high.
  • Usage, chatbot choice and attachments all sit in the same cramped footer.

The familiar AI-chat layout: a roomy history sidebar on the left and one calm, centered conversation. The top bar goes away, and the page becomes the chat.

What changes

  • Top navigation bar replaced by a sidebar; Attendance, People and Settings move to a small "Go to" row at the bottom.
  • Chatbot picker becomes a pill in the header and a chip in the composer.
  • Token usage moves to the sidebar footer as a quiet bar.

Spacing

  • Message text 17 px at 1.8 line height, 36 px between messages.
  • Conversation column 760 px, composer 112 px tall and rounded to 28 px.
  • Sidebar 304 px, rows 44 px or taller.

Trade-offs

  • Admin pages need a different frame (a slim rail works, see idea 2).
  • On phones the sidebar becomes a slide-in drawer.
  • Least new design to build, because the content stays the same.

What changes

  • Global navigation becomes an icon rail with labels, always visible.
  • History gets its own column, so it never covers the chat.
  • Files, tokens and the chatbot move into a panel that can be hidden.

Spacing

  • Rail 84 px, list 328 px, context 336 px, chat gets the rest.

Trade-offs

  • Good frame for People, Agents and Reports too.

What changes

  • The first screen asks what you want to do and who should help.
  • History moves to its own page; recent chats appear as cards.
  • Opening a chat gives a full-width conversation like idea 4.

Spacing

  • Hub content 980 px wide with 22 px between blocks.
  • Assistant cards are 24 px rounded with an avatar and a plain-language description.

Trade-offs

  • Adds one extra screen to build and maintain.
  • Great for onboarding; daily users may want a shortcut straight to the last chat.
  • On phones the cards stack and the composer stays pinned.

What changes

  • The sidebar becomes a drawer opened from the clock button or Ctrl K search.
  • The chatbot switcher sits in the top bar, always visible.
  • Follow-up suggestions appear above the composer.

Spacing

  • Conversation up to 800 px, centered when the drawer is closed.
  • Top bar 76 px; the rest of the screen belongs to the chat.

Trade-offs

  • Cleanest reading experience; history is one click further away.
  • Phone layout is nearly the same as desktop, so the least extra work.
  • Admin pages share the top bar and use full-width pages.

What changes

  • Anything the AI writes as a document, or any file you attach, opens in the canvas instead of scrolling away in the chat.
  • History becomes a "Chats" dropdown in the chat header.

Spacing

  • Chat column 580 px; canvas takes the rest and can collapse to give the chat the full width.
  • The canvas stays closed for plain conversations.

Trade-offs

  • Biggest build: the generated-file support (PDF, Word, Excel output) is still waiting on the Hermes file-transfer decision.
  • Phones show the canvas as a full-screen sheet over the chat.
  • Could be added later on top of idea 1, as a slide-in panel.

My suggestion

Start with idea 1 (Focus) for the chat screen: it fixes the cramped feeling with the least new work, and employees will recognise it at once.

Borrow two things: the chatbot cards from idea 3 on the New chat screen (so General and Coach are obvious), and the slim icon rail from idea 2 as the frame for admin pages like People, Agents and Settings.

The canvas from idea 5 can come later, when generated documents are ready. Tell me which idea (or mix) you like and I'll build it in the real app, keeping the brand rules.
